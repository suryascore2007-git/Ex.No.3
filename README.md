### Conclusion
Prompt Engineering is the process of designing effective inputs (prompts) for Artificial Intelligence systems to generate accurate, meaningful, and relevant outputs. Different prompting techniques help improve the quality of responses depending on the task, context, and expected outcome. This report explains four major prompting techniques with suitable scenario-based examples.

## 1. Straightforward Prompts

**Definition**

Straightforward prompting is the simplest prompting method where instructions are given directly and clearly without additional structure or context.

**Scenario**

A student wants an AI tool to generate a short blog introduction on remote work.

**Prompt Example**

"Write a 150-word blog introduction on the benefits of remote work."

**AI Output Summary**

The AI generates a concise introduction explaining how remote work offers flexibility, reduced commute time, and better work-life balance, while also benefiting companies through lower costs and wider talent access.

**Advantages**
- Simple and easy to create
- Saves time
- Suitable for general tasks

**Limitations**
- Output may lack depth
- Less control over formatting and detail

## 2. Tabular Format Prompting

**Definition**

Tabular prompting structures information in rows and columns to obtain organized and comparative outputs.

**Scenario**

A content writer wants to compare blog writing and article writing before choosing a format.

**Prompt Example**

"Compare and contrast blog writing and article writing in a table."

**Example Output**

| Aspect | Blog Writing | Article Writing |
|--------|-------------|------------------|
| Tone | Casual, conversational | Formal, informative |
| Length | 500–1000 words | 800–2000 words |
| Purpose | Engage and build audience connection | Inform or educate readers |
| Structure | Flexible, personal voice | Structured, often research-backed |

**Advantages**
- Produces organized information
- Easy to compare data
- Improves readability

**Limitations**
- Not suitable for creative writing
- Complex prompts may confuse structure

## 3. Missing Word Prompting

**Definition**

Missing word prompting provides incomplete sentences where the AI predicts or fills the missing information based on context.

**Scenario**

A content writing student wants to revise key terminology using fill-in-the-blank practice.

**Prompt Example**

"A short, catchy opening line used to hook a reader is called a ______."

**Expected AI Output**

"hook"

**Another Example**

"In content writing, the process of editing for grammar and clarity is called ______."

**Output:** "proofreading"

**Advantages**
- Useful for education and quizzes
- Improves learning interaction
- Enhances contextual understanding

**Limitations**
- Works only with contextual clues
- May produce multiple valid answers

## 4. Preceding Question Prompting

**Definition**

Preceding question prompting involves asking related preliminary questions before the main prompt to guide the AI toward a more accurate response.

**Scenario**

A student wants a well-reasoned explanation of why blog structure matters for reader engagement.

**Prompt Example**

Question 1: "Why do readers lose interest in long-form content?" 
Question 2: "What makes a report different from a blog post?" 
Main Prompt: "Explain how content structure and tone should adapt based on the type of content being written."

**AI Output Summary**

The AI first establishes that readers lose interest when content isn't broken into scannable sections, causing fatigue and drop-off. It then explains that reports differ from blogs in tone and structure, being more formal and data-driven. Building on this context, it concludes that writers should adapt structure, tone, and pacing according to content type — using short paragraphs and subheadings for blogs, and structured, formal sections for reports.

**Advantages**
- Produces detailed and accurate outputs
- Helps AI understand context better
- Improves logical flow of responses

**Limitations**
- Time-consuming
- Requires multiple prompts
